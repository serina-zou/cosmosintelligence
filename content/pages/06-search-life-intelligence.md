# Searching for life and intelligence

One of humanity's most profound questions is also one of its simplest:

**Are we alone?**

CosmosIntelligence treats the search for life and intelligence as a scientific and computational challenge that can connect astronomy, astrobiology, SETI, AI, citizen science and future sensing technologies.

## Biosignatures

Biosignatures are observations that may be consistent with biological activity. Examples can include atmospheric chemistry, chemical disequilibrium, surface features or combinations of measurements that are difficult to explain through known nonbiological processes.

No single intriguing signal should automatically be called evidence of life. Context and multiple lines of evidence matter.

## Technosignatures

Technosignatures are possible signs of technology. Searches may examine radio signals, optical signals, unusual energy patterns, atmospheric chemistry, artificial light, waste heat, orbital behavior or other observations that could have technological explanations.

Natural processes and instrument effects can mimic unusual signals. The evidence ladder must remain strict.

## From SETI to multimodal intelligence search

Traditional SETI has often focused on radio and optical searches. Those remain important. AI makes it practical to compare more forms of information at the same time.

A future system could combine radio, spectra, images, light curves, atmospheric measurements, orbital information, high energy observations and scientific literature. The goal is not to make extraordinary claims easier. It is to make the search more comprehensive.

## Exoplanets as a participation path

Exoplanets create a powerful bridge between public exploration and real research. Users can discover a world in Space Buddy, review its known properties through the NASA Exoplanet Archive, learn how transit measurements work and then move into a citizen science project such as NASA Exoplanet Watch.

## Could we ever communicate?

If humanity eventually discovers credible evidence of a technological civilization, the next question would be whether meaningful communication is possible.

The distances involved are enormous and the technology used by another civilization may be unfamiliar. Communication might take years, centuries or longer. It may use physical principles or channels we have not yet learned to exploit.

Advances in AI, quantum science, photonics, neuroscience, signal processing, sensors and autonomous systems may eventually create detection or communication methods we cannot currently predict.

This is a research frontier, not a promise.

## What CosmosIntelligence can contribute

The project can build source maps, data connectors, anomaly review systems, citizen science pathways, multimodal models and interdisciplinary research notes. It can also create an environment where people with very different backgrounds can work on the same big question without lowering the standards of evidence.
