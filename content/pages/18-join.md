# Build CosmosIntelligence and Space Buddy with us

CosmosIntelligence is designed as a real research and product initiative where people learn by contributing to work that can become public, testable and useful.

You do not need to fit one technical profile.

## Ways to contribute

Work includes AI and machine learning, astronomy, software engineering, data engineering, 3D and XR, product design, research, citizen science, business development, partnerships, marketing, search and AI discovery, science communication, education, community, media, analytics, operations, funding research, localization, accessibility, legal and compliance research, project management and other disciplines that help the work advance.

[Explore all roles](/join/roles/)

## What contributors can produce

Examples include research briefs, source maps, integration tests, prototypes, benchmark datasets, AI evaluations, visualizations, user research, partner maps, outreach materials, demo videos, citizen science walkthroughs, community programs, marketing experiments and published research notes.

## Start where you are

Beginners can begin with structured work such as source review, testing, documentation, data labeling, content and guided research tasks.

More experienced contributors can own methods, models, architecture, research review, partnerships, product work or team coordination.

## No fixed timeline

The public build map is organized around work areas and completion signals rather than arbitrary dates. Contributors can join where their skills match an open need.

[See the Product Requirements and Build Map](/product/space-buddy/build-map/)

## A starter challenge

A useful onboarding assignment is to choose one object, mission, audience, research question or integration and produce a small useful artifact: a source map, connector test, guided experience, research note, interface concept, outreach brief, citizen science walkthrough or other reviewable output.

## Why this project is different

The same initiative connects frontier research, public education and product development. Someone researching an exoplanet can help improve the Cosmic Graph, write an explainer, test Space Buddy and build a citizen science pathway around the same topic.

That creates a direct connection between learning and contribution.
