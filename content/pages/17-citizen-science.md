# From curiosity to contribution

Space Buddy should not stop after explaining the Universe. When a real research opportunity exists, it should help the user take the next step.

## A participation path

**Explore:** discover an object, mission or question.

**Learn:** understand the relevant science and evidence.

**Observe:** use your own equipment, a remote telescope or archived data when available.

**Analyze:** follow the official project's tools and methods.

**Contribute:** submit results through the official research workflow.

**Share:** document what you learned and help the next participant.

## NASA Exoplanet Watch

NASA Exoplanet Watch is a strong example because anyone can participate. People with telescopes can observe transiting exoplanets. People without telescopes can request or analyze data. NASA provides tutorials and the EXOTIC analysis workflow.

[NASA Exoplanet Watch](https://science.nasa.gov/citizen-science/exoplanet-watch/)

Space Buddy can explain what a transit is, show the planet, retrieve supporting information from the Exoplanet Archive and then hand the user into the official Exoplanet Watch workflow.

## More citizen science

The project can maintain a vetted registry of astronomy and space related citizen science programs. Each entry should identify the official operator, prerequisites, task type, data source, contribution method and whether participants can receive recognition.

## Telescope pathways

Future telescope integrations can connect users to partner or public observation resources where documented access or formal arrangements exist. Space Buddy should never assume access to a telescope network without a supported interface or agreement.

## Citizen scientists can also build CosmosIntelligence

Citizen scientists can test guided research flows, review data, help document methods, classify observations, evaluate usability and contribute research notes.
